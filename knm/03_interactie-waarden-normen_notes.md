# KNM A2 – Hoofdstuk 3: Interactie, Waarden en Normen

## 1. Omgang in Nederland (Interaction in the Netherlands)

### Key Facts
- Lesson covers: greetings, addressing people, visiting, gifts, cards, queuing, public transport, directness, appointments, living arrangements
- Dutch directness: Nederlanders zeggen wat ze denken ("direct communiceren") — honesty is valued over politeness; directness saves time and prevents misunderstandings
- Regional variation: Limburg = friendly/careful; Amsterdam = fast/direct
- Age: young people say "jij/je"; older people tend to use "u"
- Social class: higher educated = more formal at work; lower educated = more informal
- Work culture: healthcare (zorg) = warm; construction (bouw) = direct and brief
- Civil servants (ambtenaren) in their official role may NOT accept gifts

### Addressing People (Aanspreken)
- "U" = used for elderly people, sometimes for a boss
- "Jij/je" = used for most people, even strangers — the default in the Netherlands
- Most Dutch people say "jij/je" even to people they don't know

### Greetings (Begroeten)
- **Handshake (handgeven)**: between men/women and women/men when introducing yourself; also used on birthdays (say "gefeliciteerd") and when someone has died (say "gecondoleerd")
- **Three kisses (drie zoenen)**: informal greeting between a man and woman, or between two women — usually NOT between two men

### Visiting (Op bezoek gaan)
- Always make an appointment in advance — impulsive drop-ins are uncommon
- Arrive on time: up to 10–15 minutes late is acceptable; 30 minutes or more is NOT normal
- Dinner invitations are always arranged in advance — impulsive staying to eat is rare

### Gifts (Cadeautjes geven)
| Occasion | Appropriate gift |
|---|---|
| Visiting someone / party | Flowers, a bottle of wine, or something tasty (e.g. chocolates) |
| Birthday | A present |
| NOT appropriate | Gifts to a civil servant (ambtenaar) in their official capacity |

### Cards (Kaartjes sturen)
| Occasion | Action |
|---|---|
| Someone is ill | Send "beterschap wensen" card |
| Birthday | Send a card |
| Someone has died | Send a "sterkte wensen" or condolence card to the family |

### Scenario Rules

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| In a queue (supermarkt, gemeentehuis) | Wait your turn — cutting in line (voordringen) is considered very rude |
| Waiting with a number (gemeentehuis) | Take a number from the machine; wait until your number appears on screen |
| In a waiting room (e.g. dokter) | Don't make phone calls — go outside if you need to call |
| In public transport | Always carry a vervoersbewijs and ID |
| In stiltecoupe on the train | No talking allowed |
| Elderly/disabled person has no seat | Offer your seat |
| Neighbor plays music too loud | First: speak to them directly. If that fails: call the police |
| Problem with a colleague | First: resolve it directly with the person. If that fails: speak to your manager |
| Not sure how to behave | Observe, adapt where needed, ask a colleague/teacher/buurt team member |
| Unsure about local customs | Ask "Hoe doen jullie dat hier?" or "Zeggen mensen hier u of jij?" |

### Types of Living Arrangements (Woonvormen)

| Type | Description | Legal Rights |
|---|---|---|
| Getrouwd (huwelijk) | Legal contract between two people | Automatic rights: inheritance (erfenis), pension, parental authority (ouderlijk gezag) |
| Ongehuwd samenwonen | Living together without marriage; may have samenlevingscontract | No automatic legal rights — must be recorded via notaris |
| Geregistreerd partnerschap | Like marriage but no ceremony; legally recognised | Same rights and duties as marriage |
| Gezin | Two adults + one or more children | — |
| Eenoudergezin | One parent + children | — |

- ~57% of people in the Netherlands live in a gezin
- Same-sex couples can cohabit and marry (since **2001**)

## 2. Een Sociaal Netwerk Opbouwen (Building a Social Network)

### Key Facts
- A social network helps with: finding work/internship, getting help with problems, feeling at home, learning language and customs
- Larger network = more opportunities and support

### Types of X — Ways to Build Your Network

| Channel | Description |
|---|---|
| Vereniging | Join a sports club (voetbal) or cultural club (zangvereniging); members often volunteer to help |
| Buurthuis | Community center: coffee, Dutch practice, buurtfeest, spelletjesavond, taalkursus |
| Vrijwilligerswerk | Voluntary/unpaid work for an organisation or others |
| Children's clubs | When your children join a club, you also meet other parents |

### Vrijwilligerswerk (Voluntary Work)
- Examples: sportclub/school helper, buurthuis koffie, taalmaatje, wandelen met ouderen
- Benefits: practice Dutch, learn customs, make friends, show you want to participate, can sometimes lead to paid work
- Start small: a few hours per week or month is fine

### Scenario Rules

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Want to find vrijwilligerswerk | Ask at gemeente/wijkcentrum, or check vrijwilligerswerk.nl / nov.nl / gemeente website, or ask a sportclub/buurthuis directly |
| Neighbor causes overlast (e.g. loud music) | First: talk to them directly. After 2–3 attempts without result: call the police |
| You are causing overlast (e.g. throwing a party) | Tell your neighbors in advance; optionally invite them |

## 3. Feest- en Familiedagen (Celebrations and Special Days)

### Key Facts
- Dutch respect other cultures' feast days (Ramadan, Holi) — all are accepted

### Official Public Holidays

| Feestdag | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kerst (Christmas) | 24–25 december | Christian; celebrating birth of Jesus; 2 weeks school holiday; gifts; also celebrated by non-believers |
| Pasen (Easter) | Sunday + Monday in March or April | Christian; resurrection of Jesus; Monday is a free day; also non-believers |
| Koningsdag (King's Day) | 27 april | Birthday of King Willem-Alexander; free day; music, games, orange decorations, vrijmarkt |
| Bevrijdingsdag (Liberation Day) | 5 mei | Liberation from Germany in WWII (1945); free day **once every 5 years**; school vacation around this period |
| Dodenherdenking | 4 mei | Commemoration of WWII dead; day before Bevrijdingsdag |
| Pinksteren (Pentecost) | Usually May | Christian; free days |
| Hemelvaart (Ascension Day) | Usually May | Christian; free days |
| Oud en nieuw (New Year's Eve/Day) | 31 december & 1 januari | Traditions: oliebollen eten, vuurwerk afsteken; 1 januari is a free day |

### Culturally Relevant (Not Official) Celebrations

| Feest | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sinterklaas | 5 december | Christian origin; NOT an official free day; children believe Sinterklaas comes from Spain by boat; tradition: schoentje zetten; adults also give gifts to each other |
| Carnaval | February or March | Catholic origin; big festival in Limburg, Brabant, parts of Gelderland; lasts 3 days; costumes, processions, music; not official |
| Suikerfeest | First day of 10th Islamic calendar month | Islamic feast after Ramadan; family, food, new clothes; not official but increasingly recognised at schools |
| Keti Koti | 1 juli | Commemoration of abolition of slavery in Suriname and Caribbean; freedom/remembrance; growing recognition, especially in Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Den Haag |

### Types of Special Family Days

| Event | Key Details |
|---|---|
| Verjaardag (birthday) | Friends/family invited; cadeautjes; jarige brings taart to work (trakteren); children trakteer at school; say "gefeliciteerd" |
| Geboorte (birth) | Parents send kaartjes with name and birth date; family/friends visit a few days later (kraamvisite); tradition: beschuit met muisjes (pink for girl, blue for boy) |
| Huwelijk (wedding) | Bruiloft held; formal marriage at gemeentehuis; optional church ceremony; receptie to congratulate the couple; honeymoon (huwelijksreis) after |
| Geslaagd (graduation) | Dutch tradition: hang schooltas aan de vlaggenstok; party with friends |
| Begrafenis/Crematie | Nabestaanden send a kaart; attend uitvaart (in kerk or crematorium); wear dark clothing; say "gecondoleerd" or "sterkte wensen" |

## 4. Nederlandse Waarden en Normen (Dutch Values and Norms)

### Key Facts
- **Waarden** = things considered important in a group (e.g. freedom of speech)
- **Normen** = rules that result from those values (e.g. discrimination is forbidden)
- Grondwet states: discrimination is forbidden; all people are equal and have the same rights
- Honesty (eerlijkheid) is valued more than politeness (beleefdheid)

### Core Dutch Values

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vrijheid van meningsuiting | Everyone may say what they think (freedom of expression) |
| Vrijheid van godsdienst | Everyone may believe in any religion |
| Gelijkheid (equality) | Men and women: same professions, same chances at school and job applications |
| Respect | For people with different ideas, customs, religions |

### Gender Equality Specifics
- Men and women can do the same professions
- Same chances at school and during job applications
- Most women continue working after the birth of a child
- Men and women share household tasks (koken, schoonmaken)

### What is Accepted
- Homosexual relationships and same-sex marriage (since 2001)
- Public display of affection (hand holding, kissing) — also for same-sex couples
- Freedom to choose your own clothing (bare arms, bare legs = not forbidden)
- Public display of being gay/lesbian

### What is Forbidden

| Forbidden | Notes |
|---|---|
| All forms of violence | Includes: huiselijk geweld, eerwraak, besnijdenis van vrouwen, verkrachting |
| Discrimination | Forbidden by the Grondwet |
| Carrying weapons | Always forbidden |

### Scenario Rules

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| You are a victim of violence | Report to Slachtofferhulp Nederland or the politie |
| Someone you know is a victim of violence | Report to Slachtofferhulp Nederland or the politie |
| Someone discriminates against you | You can report to the anti-discriminatiebureau (from previous chapter) |
